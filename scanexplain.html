Content Scanning in DosGato

Scanners are separate services that check our content and report problems back to DosGato. DosGato stores what they find, shows it to editors, and lets administrators decide which scanners run where. The screens below are sketches, not final designs.

What kinds of scans are possible

Scanners can check pages, data entries, and assets. They run on live sites and on sandboxes, so a site can be checked before it launches, and when a sandbox is promoted its results and editor responses carry over. Archives are never scanned.

StalenessAn AI model reads a page after it's edited and flags content likely to go out of date, each with a suggested expiration date: step-by-step instructions for third-party software, explicit dates ("Register by March 1"), figures that change every year like tuition and fees. Nobody sees anything until that date arrives. The scanner can also add a whole-page "due for its yearly review" finding to every page, regardless of what the AI says.
AccessibilityWCAG checks on rendered pages. A problem that can't be traced to its exact section is attached to a larger section around it.
Broken linksLinks that 404, redirect, or time out.
Content qualityAI checks for meaningless alt text, plain language, brand style, inclusive language.
Sensitive dataStudent IDs or SSNs that shouldn't be on a public page.

How a staleness finding plays out

A naive scanner would re-read the page after every edit and push the expiration date out forever. Because we track each flagged section by its content rather than its position, edits elsewhere on the page don't touch the date. Only editing the flagged section itself starts over.

When the editor rewrites the flagged section

Editing the flagged section itself is the other way a finding ends. The old flag goes away at the next scan, and the AI judges the new text from scratch, so the old date no longer applies.

Any edit to the section counts, even a small one. Someone who edited it had the content in front of them, so if they left the time-sensitive part alone, that's good evidence it's still accurate. If the AI decides the new text isn't likely to go stale, there's simply no new finding.

Badges in the page tree

The page tree is busy already, so scanning adds as little as possible: a small badge at the right edge of the Name column on pages that have open findings, next to the existing page-tags indicator, and nothing at all on every other page. The badge counts open findings and takes the color of the most severe one. Hovering it shows the count for each severity. Everything else about a finding lives on the edit page.

Findings whose date hasn't arrived, or that an editor has verified or dismissed, don't count.

One open question: when a collapsed page has no findings of its own but some of its subpages do, nothing in the tree hints at them. We don't have a design for that yet.

Findings in the page editor

Components aren't wrapped in the editor, they just have an edit bar above them, and edit bars are already color coded by depth. So the only addition to the page itself is a warning icon on the edit bar of each flagged component, with a tooltip summarizing its findings.

The full list goes in a panel that slides out over the actions panel, opened with a new "View Findings" action and closed again when the editor is done. Selecting a finding there scrolls to its component, and it works the other way too: clicking the warning icon on an edit bar opens the findings panel and highlights and scrolls to that component's finding.

What sticks and what resets

  • Moving a section around the page keeps its findings and any responses.
  • Editing the section clears its findings and responses; the next scan judges the new content.
  • Verifying again later defaults to whatever length the editor picked last time, so a 30-day guess on a tuition table that changes yearly only has to be corrected once.
  • Responding requires edit permission on the page.

Findings on an asset

Asset findings always apply to the whole file, so there's no component to mark. They get their own panel on the asset's detail page, with the same buttons. Uploading a replacement file starts its findings over; editing the title or alt text doesn't.

Managing scanners

A scanner can't post anything until it's registered, either here or automatically by a software release that ships one. That keeps an unapproved service from putting alerts in front of editors. This page needs a new "manage scanners" permission, which superusers get by default. Scanners report for themselves which kinds of content they check.

The Scanning panel on a site

Each site's detail page gets a Scanning panel next to Authorized Templates. Administrators can exempt the site from all scanning, exempt it from one scanner, or turn on an opt-in scanner. Site managers see the panel but can't change it, because accessibility scanning is a compliance tool and a site shouldn't be able to waive it for itself.

Out of scope

Future enhancements

  • Trend chartsEach scanner's summary numbers are kept for 2 years, so charts could be added later, but none are planned.
  • Subtree counts in the page, asset, and data treesShowing that a collapsed page or folder has open findings somewhere beneath it, even when it has none of its own. We don't have a design for it yet.
  • A "rescan now" buttonScanners decide their own schedules for now. We may add a way to request a rescan later, for scanners that support it.

Likely never

  • Sandbox, page, or folder controls in the Scanning panelExemptions there are per site. Whether a scanner runs on sandboxes is left to the scanner: the staleness scanner will simply never scan them, while others might scan the live site regularly and sandboxes on request, once we can trigger scans. For a single page, editors can dismiss or verify findings, or a page template can offer a setting like "Don't review this page for staleness" that the scanner checks. Editors control that setting, so it only suits scanners where they should have the choice, never accessibility.
  • Dismissing something everywhere at onceThat belongs in the scanner's own tool, like the link checker's list of false positives.
  • Scanning archives
  • Problems that span pagesLike two pages giving different deadlines for the same event. Every finding belongs to one page.
